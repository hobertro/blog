## HUMAN MOMENT 09.27.24

A few weeks ago, a woman got her car stuck on the sidewalk of the street at the car wash. I tried to help get her car off the sidewalk, but the way the car was positioned required something more than just pushing or pulling the car out of its stuck position. Some of the patrons of the car wash noticed us struggling to get the car off the sidewalk and proceeded to help us. With their help, we were able to successfully to help the woman and get her car unstuck. I was so grateful for their help but I did not get to properly thank them.

Today I recognized one of those patrons, who was an older, hispanic gentleman and I decided I wanted to repay his kindness by giving him a complimentary car wash. He was surprised and asked "Why?" But he immediately realized that it must be because I recognized him as one of the people that helped me with the woman's car, and humbly accepted. As I was about to turn on the car wash for him, I turned around to see tears coming down his face. 

I was a little taken back and I asked if he was okay. He said his dog had just passed away, and said something in Spanish that I couldn't really understand, but his gestures made me believe that it was something a long the lines that he wanted to have a clean car in memory of his dog (or something like that). We shared a short moment of silence and then I told him I am sorry for his loss. 

After he was done with the car wash, he came to thank me while wiping away tears. Being a dog owner myself, I felt really sad, and wanted to go home and spend time with my own dog. 

While driving home, I thought about all the human moments that occurred for us to get to the moment: The patrons at the car wash noticed that two people were struggling and decided to help. Because they helped, I repaid that kindness with what I thought was the least minimum thing I could do. Through acts of kindness, we were able to meet at moment where we could share an important and vunerable part of ourselves, which was loss and love for our dogs.
